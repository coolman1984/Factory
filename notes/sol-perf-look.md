### 2. Motion: deterministic story-time schedules (player.js)

Contract (all pure functions of story time, nothing depends on frame history):

- `cleanPath(path)`: drops duplicate points (< `MIN_SEG`=1e-4) and removes the middle point of same-direction collinear triples (relative cross < 1e-6). A straight line never stops.
- `buildSchedule(path, headingIn)` -> `{ pts, phases[], duration, length, h0, h1 }`. Phases: `move` (one straight segment) and `turn` (in place). `headingIn` undefined means "set my own initial heading, no turn". Heading convention is the model's `rotation.y = atan2(dx, dy)` (slide x, slide y).
- Move profile: speed `V_MAX = 1.6 m/s` (metres via `PT_M`), ramp `RAMP_T = 1.2 s` with a smoothstep speed ramp (position `vp*Ta*(x^3 - x^4/2)`), cruise, mirrored ramp; rest at both ends. Segments shorter than `V_MAX*RAMP_T = 1.92 m` use ramp time `RAMP_T*sqrt(L/1.92)` (same acceleration, lower peak, e.g. 0.3 m takes about 0.95 s). Segment time = `2*Ta + (L-1.92)/1.6` when long enough.
- Turn: `TURN_T = 0.8 s` per 90 degrees (scaled by |angle|/90), `smoothstep` ease in-out, position fixed at the corner (zero translation), delta wrapped to (-180, 180] and snapped to exactly +-90 / 180 within 1e-6. At turn end heading is returned as exactly the next segment heading; during moves it is exactly `atan2(dx, dy)`.
- A step's first phase is a turn from the previous step's final heading, so there is no heading snap at step boundaries; stationary and lift steps keep the heading; leading stationary steps take the first move's heading. Lift step is unchanged (3.2 s, eased lerp).
- Step duration: `tm - t0 = sum of phase times` (no cap, no global ease); `t1 = tm + DWELL(1.8)`. A stationary step is 0.6 s. Items keep `s, i, path, t0, tm, t1, metres, run` and gain `sched, base (metres before this step), h0, h1`.
- `sampleSchedule(sched, tau, out)` -> `out.x,y` (slide points), `h`, `d` (distance along the cleaned route, slide points), `v` (m/s), `k` (`move` | `turn` | `end`), `seg`. Seekable, allocation-free (reused `out`).
- Exposed on `window.MF` for synthetic checks: `MF.schedule`, `MF.sampleSchedule`, `MF.cleanPath`, `MF.lotPose(t)`, `MF.motion` ({V_MAX, RAMP_T, TURN_T}), `MF.PT_M`, `MF.vehicleAPI`, `MF.LOOK`, `MF.route` ({uniforms, flows, lifts, liftState, plateOf}). Previous hooks unchanged: floors, S, D, REP, W, proj, seek, play, pause, stepBy, setFlow, setView, setCam, setLook, setPlan, setMeasure, setQuality, setFloorLight, debugFloor, stepAt, frame, want, perf, ready.
- Lot: `lot.rotation.y = lotPos.h` every frame (nearby-sample heading removed).
- Vehicles (lead-owned): the player **no longer overrides `REP.live`**; the earlier conveyor/vehicle override with nearby-sample headings is gone. `MF.vehicleAPI = typeof REP.vehicleAt === "function"`. If present, `REP.live(time, R, dt)` gets raw story time (no .55 factor). If absent (the case in the replica.js I read: no `vehicleAt`), the original `REP.live(time*.55, R, dt*.55)` is called exactly as before. Cost: the earlier allocation-free conveyor optimisation was removed with the override because replica's own `live` also animates conveyors; it can return if the lead exposes a vehicles-only entry point. Forklift camera reads `m.heading ?? m.v.rotation.y`.
- Guided camera: azimuth is constant per story step (`AZ_STEP[i % 6]`, smoothed with rate 1.3 /s so a step change moves it slowly); no time-varying term. Target = lot blended 65% toward the middle of the current segment (the next segment during a turn), distance = segment length x 1.35 clamped to 0.85-1.8x the step distance. Eye and target springs use omega 3.2 (guided). Corners do not rotate the camera. Free / presets / follow / chase (now uses the schedule heading) / forklift / test API are preserved.

### 3. Route light (player.js)

- The canvas flow overlay is no longer the 3D route. Per flow and plate there is one static `ShaderMaterial` ribbon mesh (width 3.6 points, y=0.6, orthogonal quads overlapping at joints) and one station-ring mesh, parented to `plates[key]` so stack, floor offsets and elevator landing follow plate transforms automatically.
- Plate assignment (correction): each ribbon segment is split at the plate boundaries (`PLATE_X = 553`; for x >= 553 also `PLATE_Y = 303`, the same constants `plateOf` uses) and every portion goes to the plate it lies on, so a route crossing a boundary follows both plates when floors stack. Adjacent portions on the same plate merge back; `aU` stays the global distance (`cum + t * len * PT_M`), and the joint overlap (`hw`) is applied only at the segment's real corners, not at the plate seam. Paths, data and layout are untouched. I did not read `private/`, so I make **no claim about which actual routes cross a boundary**; the splitting is general and applies to any that do. Batching by plate is kept (still one ribbon mesh per plate and flow).
- Station rings (correction): one ring per real step: the end of its path (for a lift, the upper landing), the **lower landing as well for a lift**, and the node position of **pathless steps** (pickup/drop-off, previously skipped). Rings are not de-duplicated (a step that starts where the previous one ended gets its own ring at the same spot, so "current" pulses on the right step).
- Elevator connector (correction): a lift step used to have no 3D route light at all. Now each lift step has one connector mesh named `MF route lift flow <flow> step <step>`, added to the scene (not to a plate: it spans two plates) and listed in `MF.route.flows[i].meshes`. Geometry is shared and static: two crossed strips (3.6 wide, one in local X, one in local Y) from local z 0 to 1, with `aU` 0..1 and `aV` -1..1. Each frame `updateRoute` only sets the mesh's position (= `W(E.low)`), quaternion (towards `W(E.high)`) and `scale.z` (= distance), from the same `liftLow`/`liftHigh` vectors `frame()` uses for the cabin and shaft, so it follows the stack including partial values. Same fragment program as the floor ribbon (chevrons that point low to high, soft halo, same `uGain`, `uAlpha`, `uDim`, normal blending, no depth write, no new pass); one `ShaderMaterial` per lift step (identical GLSL, so one compiled program) holds its own `uDone`/`uHead`/`uLen`. `uLen` = length x `PT_M` metres. Progress: the lift step's `lp.lift` (the same eased value that moves the lot) while current, 1 once the step is past, 0 before. `uDone` = `progress * uLen` while travelling (the bright head sits at the lot), `-0.5` before (all dim) and `uLen + 0.5` after (all bright); `uHead` is 1 only while 0 < progress < 1. Visibility follows the other route meshes (route alpha and the active flow). No allocation or buffer upload per frame.
- Diagnostics: `MF.route.lifts` (flat list of entries `{ flow, step, item, mesh, material, from, to, progress, state, length }`, `from`/`to` copied from the world ends each frame while that flow is shown), `MF.route.liftState()` (allocating, for tests: per connector `meshFrom`/`meshTo` rebuilt from the mesh transform, `expectedFrom`/`expectedTo` = `W(E.low)`/`W(E.high)` at the current stack, `progress`, `state` = future | current | past, `uLen`, `uDone`, `uHead`), and `MF.route.plateOf`. Mesh ends are from the transform set by the last `frame()`, so call it after `MF.frame`/`seek` for the flow being checked.
- Shared uniforms (`routeU`): done distance, story time, step, gain, alpha. `aU` is the flow-global route distance in metres, `done = item.base + schedule d * PT_M`, so travelled/future is exact schedule distance, not a time fraction. No per-frame geometry change, upload or allocation. All flows are built once at startup (a flow switch only toggles `visible`); draw calls are the ribbon and ring meshes of the shown flow (at most a few each, one per plate) plus one per lift connector in that flow.
- Look: orange emissive core with soft halo and slow forward chevrons (tip leads along the route), travelled bright, future dim (`uDim` 0.3), brighter head. Stations: thin ring per step end, done = medium, future = faint, current = pulsing expanding wave and soft disc. Uses the renderer's tone mapping and output colour chunks, normal alpha blending, no depth write, no extra pass. `uGain` is 1 (Studio), 1.2 (Blueprint), 3.2 (Night). Night was 2.4 in the first edit; that was a false bloom claim: the bloom luminance threshold is 2.2 and the brightest ribbon colour is RGB (1, .78, .36) x gain, luma (.299, .587, .114) about 0.796 x gain, so 2.4 gives a maximum luma of about 1.91 and no route fragment could pass. At 3.2 the maximum is about 2.55, so selective bright highlights (head, chevron peaks) can pass the threshold while the default floor can stay below it. This is **mathematical eligibility only, not GPU-validated bloom**; threshold, passes and Studio/Blueprint gains are unchanged.
- Ribbon alpha follows the rise (`smoothstep(R, .15-.85)`); the 2D canvas route (same orange style, bright up to the exact schedule distance, arrows on finished steps) fades in as the ribbon fades out, so the flat plan keeps a line.

## Evidence and findings by severity

### P0 — High misses the acceptance target; the main stall cause is not yet isolated

Owner-provided Intel Iris Xe / 1920×1080 baseline from `node tools/perf.mjs high 15`: High 24 fps average, p99 491 ms, worst 5349 ms; Medium 38 fps / worst 1977 ms; Low 67 fps / worst 1278 ms. These are baseline numbers supplied in the assignment, not results measured by this delegate.

Consequently there are no after-change GPU numbers, browser screenshots, or validated frame-time claims here. In particular, the <=22 ms median, <=50 ms p99, <=120 ms post-startup worst and <=250 ms switch budgets are **unverified**. No GPU bottleneck has been proved by an isolated timing experiment.

### P0 — Story time demonstrably discarded elapsed time

The original tick used `dt = Math.min(.1, raw)` for story advancement. A 5.349-second stall therefore advanced the story by at most 0.1 seconds. The new loop passes the full monotonic elapsed interval to `frame`; playback/seek reset its clock origin, and `frame(dt)` remains explicitly drivable for tests. No Date or wall-clock phase drives scene animation. Synthetic checks verified 1.25 seconds advances exactly 1.25, speed scaling, pause, and stopping at a step after a long frame.

### P1 — Recurring shadow and instance-buffer work: confirmed code paths, unmeasured GPU contribution

Originally a 4096×4096 PCF shadow map was dirtied each frame of rise and stack transitions. `REP.rise()` also rewrote all instance matrices. The player now installs the same sweep in the existing materials' vertex shaders and keeps the instance buffers immutable. Correct normals account for the Y scale. Synthetic checks using the real r170 material hooks verified both Standard and Basic shader injection, unchanged instance-buffer versions across rise, and the sampled sweep formula's equivalence to the original.

Default High uses no realtime sun shadow pass. `?shadows=1` opts into a 1536 map refreshed only after rise and stack settle; moving-object shadows are not continuously tracked in that optional mode. This mode is outside the default High performance claim and still needs visual review. Default floor shading uses a small cached analytic contact-light map; the lead's Blender soft shadows/AO replace it through the same uniform.

### P1 — Full-resolution multipass cost and post-chain rebuilds: confirmed allocations, stall attribution unproved

Original High used full-resolution GTAO (normal/depth geometry pass, 12 AO samples, 12 denoise samples), bloom and 4× multisampled half-float targets. `buildPost()` was called on quality changes and resize; disposing only EffectComposer also left pass-owned resources to dispose separately. Original auto-quality could enter this allocation/compile path after slow playback.

Default High now renders Studio/Blueprint directly with the existing MSAA renderer, Neutral tone mapping, PBR maps and cached environment. GTAO is removed. Night uses a single persistent non-MSAA HDR bloom/output chain at 3/4 display resolution. Quality switches and the interval governor reuse this chain; resize resizes it without recreating passes. The existing chain is retained at fixed size across quality switches. High keeps native display pixels with DPR capped at 1; Medium/Low use .8/.65. Automatic resolution reduction (.9, then .8) stays within the selected High material detail and respects a manual quality choice or pinned `?q=high`.

### P1 — First-use shader compilation and texture uploads: plausible stall source, now moved before playback

Original `setArt()` lazily drew 6 px/point plan art, uploaded it and marked floor materials for recompilation during look changes. It now uses six prebuilt/uploaded light/dark textures capped at 2048 on the long edge. `drawPlan` is lead-owned and still generates its original large temporary canvas during startup; that temporary is downsampled and released immediately. There is no plan drawing, texture creation or `needsUpdate` on a look switch.

`MF.ready` waits for original images, uploads their maps, compiles every look with both plan and PBR floors using `compileAsync` (or `compile`), renders them to a tiny offscreen target, and warms bloom/output shaders before scheduling the story loop. Startup work is recorded separately. A real driver can still have first-draw or resize stalls; this needs Chrome/GPU confirmation. Startup may take longer because work previously deferred into playback is now explicit.

### P2 — Per-frame DOM and material writes: confirmed, several suspects cleared

Original label placement assigned class names, text, display and transforms every frame and queried each label's text node repeatedly. Step card/list update and `scrollIntoView` were **already gated on a step change**, so those are cleared as every-frame offenders. The original play button HTML and formatted time were assigned every frame.

Label content/class updates now occur only on flow/step/control changes, text nodes are cached, invisible/unrelated nodes are skipped, and identical transforms/display values are not rewritten. Timeline/time/play updates are conditional; minimap runs at at most 10 Hz during playback and refreshes on flow changes. Dimming converges to its endpoint and stops writing materials. Paused movers stop uploading identical conveyor matrices. The synthetic integration harness verified **zero DOM writes over 20 settled paused frames**; this is a mutation count, not a browser timing measurement.

### P2 — Camera and movement instability / speed: confirmed implementation choices

The original guided camera varied azimuth and polar angle by step and added a live sinusoidal azimuth; a first-order chase then followed that moving target. Two analytic critically damped springs now control eye position and look target independently, preserving free-camera controls. Per-step angle oscillation is removed. A numerical check of the actual spring function verified 30 Hz, 60 Hz and one large step agree to <1e-12 for a fixed target. Moving-goal appearance still needs visual review.

The route duration's original 4.5-second maximum made long trips glide at arbitrary speed. Duration now uses 4 slide points/second (1.2 m/s average at the declared .3 m/point; smoothstep peaks at 1.8 m/s), without the upper cap. Timeline order/data remain unchanged. Ambient movers/conveyors use story time at .55 of their previous rate; a 2.6 m/s forklift becomes about 1.43 m/s. Lot and vehicle headings use nearby path samples instead of frame-history easing/snaps. Open or discontinuous routes can still jump at wrap; route geometry belongs to the lead.

## Floor bake contract for the lead

`MF.setFloorLight(plateKey, textureOrDataURI)` returns a Promise. A THREE.Texture preserves its supplied color space; a data-URI image is treated as sRGB. White means fully lit; RGB multiplies the complete floor shading after lighting. The map covers exactly `D.plates[key].box`, left-to-right/top-to-bottom like plan art, using the plane's unscaled UVs. Concrete repeats separately at a 12 m tile (joints at 6 m). Setting a bake only replaces a uniform and uploads the new texture; it does not rebuild shaders or post passes. Replaced image textures owned by the hook are disposed; caller-owned THREE.Textures remain caller-owned.

For startup, set `window.MF_FLOOR_LIGHT = { E1: dataURI, "E2-1F": dataURI, "E2-2F": dataURI }` before player.js. The current builder does not package new Blender images; that integration remains with the lead. Await `MF.ready` before starting measurements. Baked floor lighting follows the plate when stacked; it is intended for the fully risen scene. Original and clean 2D plans remain available.
